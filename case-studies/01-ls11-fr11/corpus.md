# Case study 01 — corpus: paired statements from the L&S11 and F&R11 public communications

**Status:** v0.1, 2026-10-05. Statements selected for the first paired analyses; more can be added.

## Where the statements come from

All statements are quoted verbatim from the text captures in the `climate-paper-behavior` repository (`pilot/communications/sources/`, captured 2026-09-25 by DOM text extraction, provenance headers in each file, hashes in its `MANIFEST.md`), and from the full English translations in `pilot/communications/translation-review/rahmstorf-DE_review-packet.md` (German originals authoritative). The inventory that located them is `climate-paper-discussion/public-communications-inventory-LS11-FR11.md`. Those folders are read-only for this project; nothing here changes them.

The two papers:

| Side | Paper | Authors whose public statements are used |
|---|---|---|
| LS | Loehle C. & Scafetta N. (2011), "Climate Change Attribution Using Empirical Decomposition of Climatic Data," *Open Atmospheric Science Journal* 5:74–86 | Craig Loehle, Nicola Scafetta (plus one host statement by Anthony Watts, flagged) |
| FR | Foster G. & Rahmstorf S. (2011), "Global temperature evolution 1979–2010," *Environmental Research Letters* 6, 044022 | Grant Foster ("Tamino"), Stefan Rahmstorf (plus one publisher lede, flagged) |

Typographical slips in the originals are kept (`[sic]` where a reader might otherwise stumble). Emphasis is never added.

## Conventions

- `LS-n` / `FR-n`: statement IDs. `(host)` and `(publisher)` mark statements not by a paper author; they are kept because they frame the authors' statements for readers.
- Each statement carries: author, venue, date, and the capture file it was copied from.
- Pairs (`P1`…) group statements by the **job they do** — the same rhetorical function on each side — not by topic alone. That is what makes the comparison symmetric.

## LS side — Loehle & Scafetta 2011

**LS-1** — Loehle, guest post, *Climate Etc.* (Judith Curry's blog), 2011-07-25; identical paragraph in the WUWT guest post of the same day. Capture: `2011-07-25_climate-etc_loehle_launch.txt`.

> How do we detect the influence of humans on the climate system?  Current methods based on climate models are unfortunately circular: their estimate of human effects is only valid if the models are correct, but the models make certain assumptions and also are fitted to the historical temperature record.  A model-independent estimate of climate response is needed and is provided by this study.

**LS-2** — Loehle & Scafetta, WUWT guest post, headline and sub-headline as posted, 2011-07-25. Capture: `2011-07-25_wuwt_loehle-scafetta_launch.txt`. (WUWT's own title for the post was "Loehle and Scafetta calculate 0.66°C/century for AGW".)

> Human Effect on Climate Clearly Detected
>
> (but is 0.66 deg C/100yr since ~1950!)

**LS-3** — Loehle, Conclusions item 4, *Climate Etc.* and WUWT, 2011-07-25.

> Warming due to anthropogenic GHG+Aerosol of 0.66  oC/Century is not alarming, in comparison to the IPCC protected [sic — projected] 2.3 oC/Century  This 0.66 value is an upper bound in our estimation (due to possible poorly corrected UHI and LULC effects that may explain part of the observed warming trend since 1950).

**LS-4** — Loehle, body text, *Climate Etc.* and WUWT, 2011-07-25.

> About 60% of the warming observed from 1970 to 2000 was very likely caused by this natural 60-year climatic cycle during its warming phase.

**LS-5** — Loehle, WUWT comment, 2011-07-25 12:26 pm, replying to Steven Mosher. Capture: WUWT launch file, author-comment sample.

> Mosh: You call it calibration, I call it fitting. One of the most glaring examples is the correlation between model sensitivity and the amount of sulfate aerosols assumed as input (which keeps sensitive models from running away from the data).

**LS-6** — Loehle, WUWT comment, 2011-07-25 12:24 pm.

> This is not a polynomial fit to the data. We factor out the natural cycles based on solar activity first, then identify the human signal.

**LS-7** — Loehle, forecast paragraph and Conclusions item 5, *Climate Etc.* and WUWT, 2011-07-25.

> A 21st Century forecast (below, Fig. 3) suggests that climate may remain approximately steady until 2030-2040, and may at most warm 0.5-1.0°C by 2100 at the estimated 0.66°C/century anthropogenic warming rate, which is about 3.5 times smaller than the average 2.3°C/century anthropogenic warming rate projected by the IPCC during the first decades of the 21st century.

> 5) Cooling/flat temperatures till 2030 are likely (as also predicted by others).

**LS-8** — Scafetta, "Scafetta prediction widget update," WUWT guest post, 2012-03-11. Capture: `2012-03-11_wuwt_scafetta_widget-update.txt`.

> However, it is a trick to fool people with no statistical understanding to claim that by associating a huge error range to a model, the model is validated.

**LS-9** — Scafetta, same post.

> Every smart person would understand that this is another skepticalscience's trick to fool the ignorant.

**LS-10** — Scafetta, same post (two sentences from the baseline section).

> My critics at skepticalscience are simply trying to hide the failure of the IPCC models in reconstructing the 60-year temperature modulation by just plotting the IPCC average simulation just since 2000, and by lowering the baseline apparently to the period 1960-1990, which is not where it should be because the model is supposed to reconstruct the 1900-2000 period by assumption.

> It is evident that by lowering the base line a larger divergence would be produced with the temperature data before 1960! So, skepticalscience employed a childish trick of pulling a too small coversheet from a too large bed.

**LS-11** — Scafetta, same post.

> Moreover, the claim of skepticalscience that we need to use a 2-sigma error envelope indirectly also proves that the IPCC models cannot be validated according the scientific method and, therefore, do not belong to the realm of science.

**LS-12 (host)** — Anthony Watts, "New WUWT Feature – Scafetta's forecast -vs- the IPCC forecast," 2012-02-10. Capture: `2012-02-10_wuwt_widget-announcement.txt`.

> Time will tell how well his forecast model holds up. So far it is already beating the pants off the highly upwards IPCC forecast model, but like any forecast model, isn't perfect.

**LS-13** — Scafetta, 2012-03-11, closing the section on Leif Svalgaard.

> I just would like to advise the readers of this blog, in particular those with modest scientific knowledge, to take his unfair and unprofessional comments with the proper skepticism.

**LS-14** — Scafetta, 2012-03-11, baseline section.

> In fact, the IPCC models assume that the 1970-2000 warming is 100% produced by anthropogenic forcing (compare figures 9.5a and 9.5b in the IPCC report) while the 60-year natural cycle (plus the other cycles) contributed at least 2/3 of the 1970-2000 warming, as proven in my papers.

**LS-15** — Scafetta, 2012-03-11, on the widget and on his paper's Figure 6.

> On the contrary, the data continue to be in reasonable agreement with my empirical model, which I remind, is constructed as a full forecast since Jan/2000.

> It is evident from the figure above that my model (blue curve) agrees very well with the temperature 4-year smooth component, while the IPCC projection is always worst, and statistically diverges from the temperature since 2006.

**LS-16** — Loehle, WUWT comment, 2011-07-25 4:13 pm, replying to Willis Eschenbach and Steven Mosher.

> We do not ascribe "magical properties" to a fitted curve. We observe that there is a regular orbital oscillation of the sun–this orbit pattern is fixed and calculable. We assume that this MAY somehow affect solar output/magnetic field etc. We then test if this pattern matches the temperature pattern.

## FR side — Foster & Rahmstorf 2011

**FR-1** — Foster ("Tamino"), "The Real Global Warming Signal," *Open Mind*, 2011-12-06, opening paragraph. Capture: `2011-12-06_open-mind_real-global-warming-signal.txt`.

> Many different factors affect Global temperature. Fake "skeptics" like to claim that mainstream climate scientists ignore everything but greenhouse gases like CO2, when in fact it's mainstream climate scientists who identified those other influences. Natural factors cause temperature fluctuations which make the man-made global warming signal less clear, fluctuations which are often exploited by fake skeptics to suggest that global warming has paused, or slowed down, or isn't happening at all. A new paper by Foster & Rahmstorf accounts for some of those other factors, and by removing their influence from the temperature record makes the progress of global warming much more clear.

**FR-2** — Foster, same post.

> Once the effect of the three known factors was estimated, it could be removed from the temperature data to create adjusted temperature data which is mostly (but not completely!) free of their influence.

**FR-3** — Foster, same post.

> With the bulk of the fluctuations removed, the continued course of warming over the entire time span (including the last decade) is undeniable.

**FR-4** — Foster, same post, closing lines.

> That shows, with great clarity and impact, the real global warming signal.
>
> And, it should put an end to real skeptics claiming that global warming has recently stopped or slowed down, because real skeptics base their beliefs on evidence. I don't expect it will have much effect on the behavior of fake skeptics.

**FR-5** — Foster, same post.

> This has spurred numerous false claims of silly things like "global warming stopped in 1998" (due to the large spike from the powerful el Nino of that year). Large fluctuations also make it more difficult to establish the statistical significance of trend, leading to meaningless statements about "no statistically significant warming for 15 years" (or 10 years, or 7, or since last Thursday).

**FR-6** — Foster, quoted in the IOP Publishing press release as carried by ScienceDaily, 2011-12-05. Capture: `2011-12-05_sciencedaily_iop-release.txt`.

> "The unabated warming is powerful evidence that we can expect further temperature increase in the next few decades, emphasizing the urgency of confronting the human influence on the climate," says Grant Foster, lead author of the study.

**FR-7** — Rahmstorf, same press release.

> "Our approach shows that the idea that the global warming trend has slowed or even paused over the last decade or so is a groundless misconception. It shows that differences between the five data sets reside, to a large extent, in their short-term variability and not in the climatic trend. After the variability is removed, all five data sets are very similar," said study co-author Stefan Rahmstorf.

**FR-8 (publisher)** — ScienceDaily/IOP lede, 2011-12-05.

> Researchers have added further clarity to the global climate trend, proving that global warming is showing no signs of slowing down and that further increases are to be expected in the next few decades.
>
> They revealed the true global warming trend by bringing together and analysing the five leading global temperature data sets […]

**FR-9** — Rahmstorf, "Globale Temperatur Reloaded," *KlimaLounge*, 2011-12-08. Capture: `2011-12-08_klimalounge_globale-temperatur-reloaded_DE.txt`; translation from the review packet.

> Also auch hier keinerlei Hinweise auf eine Verlangsamung oder gar Pause in der globalen Erwärmung.
>
> *So here too, no indication whatsoever of a slowdown, let alone a pause, in global warming.*

**FR-10** — Rahmstorf, "Die populärste Trickgrafik der Klimaskeptiker," *KlimaLounge*, 2012-09-27 (in the F&R11 window; the post cites F&R11 for the variability finding). Capture: `2012-09-27_klimalounge_populaerste-trickgrafik_DE.txt`; translation from the review packet.

> Die sogenannten „Klimaskeptiker" verfügen ja über ein Arsenal an irreführenden Grafiken, mit denen der Einfluss des Menschen auf das Klima heruntergespielt werden soll […]
>
> *The so-called "climate sceptics" have an arsenal of misleading graphics with which the human influence on climate is to be played down […]*

**FR-11** — Rahmstorf, same post.

> Da gehört schon einiges dazu, um aus der gezeigten Datenlage eine Grafik zu machen, die dem Laien vorgaukelt, CO2 sei unwichtig und die Sonne wichtig für die globale Temperaturentwicklung über die letzten sechzig Jahre! Wer mit dieser Grafik hausieren geht, hat sich aus jeder ernsthaften Sachdiskussion verabschiedet und den Weg der Täuschung beschritten.
>
> *It takes quite something to make, from the data shown, a graphic that deceives the layperson into believing that CO2 is unimportant and the sun important for the global temperature evolution over the last sixty years! Anyone peddling this graphic has taken leave of any serious factual discussion and taken the path of deception.*

**FR-12** — Rahmstorf, same post.

> Da staunt der Laie und der Fachmann wundert sich: Wie kann jemand, der sich als Wissenschaftler präsentiert (ich bin Chemieprofessor!) mit solchen Bauernfängertricks arbeiten?
>
> *The layperson is amazed and the expert wonders: how can someone who presents himself as a scientist ("I am a professor of chemistry!") work with such con-man tricks?*

Lexical note (not part of the quotation): *Bauernfänger*, literally "peasant-catcher," is a swindler who preys on the naïve; *vorgaukeln*, in FR-11, is built on *Gaukler*, a juggler or conjurer. The German is more specific about the con-man scenario than the English "trick" and "deceive".

**FR-13** — Foster, "We must be doing something right," *Open Mind*, 2011-12-06. Capture: `2011-12-06_open-mind_doing-something-right.txt`.

> It seems that even before the paper which is the topic of my latest post appeared, The Register attacked it. We must be doing something right.
>
> […] it looks like commenters at their site want to torpedo the truth.

**FR-14** — Rahmstorf, *KlimaLounge*, 2012-09-27, the second of two reasons given for the agreement of the CO₂ and temperature curves. Capture as FR-10; translation from the review packet.

> (2) Die natürlichen Einflüsse (Vulkane, Sonnenzyklen) auf den Trend sind verschwindend gering im Vergleich zum anthropogenen CO2 (siehe z.B. Lean & Rind 2008, Foster & Rahmstorf 2011).
>
> *(2) The natural influences (volcanoes, solar cycles) on the trend are vanishingly small compared with anthropogenic CO2 (see e.g. Lean & Rind 2008, Foster & Rahmstorf 2011).*

**FR-15** — Foster, "The Real Global Warming Signal," 2011-12-06, on the trend-rate test.

> None of the data sets shows any evidence that the global warming rate has changed recently. A truly fascinating result is that increased precision enables us to establish the statistical significance of a warming trend using a shorter time span than with unadjusted data. All five data sets show statistically significant warming since 2000.
