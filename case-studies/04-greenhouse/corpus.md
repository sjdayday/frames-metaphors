## What this case is about

Every other case in this project is about a contest over a word that one side introduced and the other adopted. This one is about a word the field itself chose, in 1901–1908, for a mechanism it had described without it since 1824; that its own reference works call a misnomer; that it has never been able to put down; and that each side now uses in its own way — "heat-trapping", "cozy blanket", "carbon pollution", "control knob", "Hothouse Earth" on one side, "so-called greenhouse gases", "not a pollutant", "plant food", "saturated", "only 1% to 2%", "no common physical laws between the warming phenomenon in glass houses" on the other. The question is what the picture does: what each of its forms presses and hides, which of the sceptical arguments are entailments of the mainstream's own picture, and what a second look can put in the picture's place.

The substance — that added CO₂ reduces outgoing infrared radiation by about 4 W m⁻² per doubling, that the saturation objection failed, and that the column warms as a whole — is `P01` (`SETTLED`), with `P05` for water vapour and `P06` for the forcing. This case does not redo it.

**Two things the trace shows before any analysis.** First, the building came late and from outside: Fourier's comparison was to a laboratory box, Tyndall's pictures were a gate, a blanket and a dam, and the "hot-house" was Arrhenius's attribution to Fourier of a word Fourier did not use; "green-house" is Ekholm's (1901), and "greenhouse effect" was born in Poynting's scare quotes (1907). Second, the picture has been disowned by the field at least four times — Wood 1909 (by experiment), the AMS glossary ("something of a misnomer"), Weart ("an inaccurate analogy"), RealClimate 2007 ("as if it were a single sheet of glass") — and is still the first sentence of the agency explainer ("heat is trapped").

## Paired statements

### S side — the sceptical and dissenting uses

**S-1** — Global Climate Coalition, 1996 (T-19): the natural "'greenhouse effect'" in scare quotes, "so-called greenhouse gases", and the effect as what "protects the Earth from the freezing rigors of space".

**S-2** — Lindzen, 1992 (T-18): "over 98 percent of the current greenhouse effect" from water vapour and clouds; the effect "only about 25 percent of what it would be in a pure radiative situation"; "the cooling hysteria had certain striking analogues to the present warming hysteria".

**S-3** — Gerlich & Tscheuschner, 2009 (T-21): "fictitious mechanism, in which a planetary atmosphere acts as a heat pump"; "such a planetary machine can never exist"; "no common physical laws between the warming phenomenon in glass houses"; "the atmospheric greenhouse conjecture is falsified."

**S-4** — Happer, 2011 (T-24): "Carbon is the stuff of life."; "not a pollutant, but part of their daily bread"; "CO2 really is a greenhouse gas"; "Other things being equal, more CO2 will cause more warming."; "0.039 percent of all atmospheric molecules".

**S-5** — Koonin, 2014 (T-25): "shift the atmosphere's natural greenhouse effect by only 1% to 2%"; "that smallness sets a very high bar".

**S-6** — CO2 Coalition, 2015– (T-27): "plant food"; "not a pollutant"; "The warming effect of each molecule of CO2 declines as its concentration increases"; "We are CO2 impoverished."; "warmer is better than colder."

**S-7** — van Wijngaarden & Happer, 2020 (T-29): "increases the forcings by a few per cent."

**S-8** — The saturation argument in its original form (T-7, Ångström 1900, as Weart and `P01` report it), kept here because every later "saturated" statement descends from it.

**S-9** — Arrhenius, 1908 (T-10): "we may hope to enjoy ages with more equable and better climates" — a mainstream founder's sentence that the sceptical side's benefit frame inherits; listed on this side for the pairing in G3 and marked as such.

## What is deliberately not here

The "carbon footprint" family (a different source domain, traced by Nerlich and Koteyko — see the literature pass), "tipping point" as a metaphor in its own right (van der Hel et al. 2018; Russill & Nyssa 2009 — likewise), and the "climate change / global warming / climate crisis" naming contest, which belongs with the framing documents (Luntz 2002; the Guardian style guide 2019) in the literature pass.
